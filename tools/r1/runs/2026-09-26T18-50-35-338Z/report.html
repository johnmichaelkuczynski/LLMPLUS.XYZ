Claude
📋

The classical libertarian view requires that free agents be the ultimate source of their choices. Pure quantum randomness in neural processes would mean some decisions trace to uncaused events, but randomness occurring to an agent differs from choices made by that agent. If a synapse fires probabilistically, that's not freedom—it's dice.

However, some libertarians explore reconciliation by proposing that quantum indeterminacy provides "causal slack" or "elbow room" that agents might exploit or harness without being reduced to it. The agent wouldn't cause events deterministically, but would non-deterministically influence probability spaces in ways that express genuine agency.

The deep challenge remains: explicating how an agent exercises control over probabilistic processes without that control itself being either (a) deterministic—collapsing back into incompatibilism's original problem—or (b) random—eliminating agency altogether. Quantum mechanics opens logical space for libertarianism by breaking causal closure, but bridging physical indeterminacy to rational, purposive agency requires a theory of agential causation that neither contemporary physics nor philosophy has fully articulated.
