What are the key differences between Kant's categorical imperative and Mill's utilitarianism in terms of moral decision-making?

------------------------------------------------------------

ASSISTANT:

**Kant: Duty-based. Act according to rules that could be universal laws, regardless of consequences.**

**Mill: Consequence-based. Act to maximize overall happiness/pleasure and minimize suffering.**

**Key difference in decision-making:**

- **Kant:** Lying is always wrong, even if it produces good outcomes — because you can't universalize "lie when convenient" without destroying trust entirely.

- **Mill:** Lying is wrong *usually* because it tends to cause more harm than good — but if a specific lie prevents greater suffering, it's morally required.

**Example:** You're hiding refugees. Authorities ask if you're harboring anyone.

- **Kant:** Tell the truth. You can't make "lie to save lives" a universal law without undermining truth-telling as an institution.
- **Mill:** Lie. The suffering prevented (refugees killed) vastly outweighs the harm of one lie.

**Kant cares about the *nature* of the act. Mill cares about the *results* of the act.**
